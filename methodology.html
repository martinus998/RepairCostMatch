COST METHODOLOGY

How our planning ranges are built.

RepairCostMatch is designed for early budgeting. It does not diagnose a structure or replace an on-site estimate. We combine published national cost guidance with the problem, severity, foundation type and common scope drivers selected by the homeowner.

1. Start with the symptom

Cracks, water intrusion, settlement and bowing walls lead to very different repair categories. The tool uses the visible symptom only as a starting point, not as a structural diagnosis.

2. Adjust for severity

Localized cosmetic work can sit in a very different price tier from widespread movement, excavation, reinforcement or pier installation.

3. Consider foundation type

Basements, slabs and crawl spaces can change access, equipment, labor and the repair methods that are practical.

4. Consider location and access

ZIP code, soil, labor rates, excavation access, permits and local construction conditions can move a real project above or below a national planning band.

5. Separate the repair method

Crack sealing, drainage, waterproofing, wall reinforcement, slab lifting, excavation and piering should not be treated as one interchangeable repair.

6. Add a safety limit

Rapid movement, visible bowing, large structural changes, active flooding or utility risk should be professionally evaluated instead of priced from a web estimate alone.

Headline cost sources

Our current foundation-repair orientation uses published 2026 consumer cost guides as reference points, including HomeAdvisor and Angi, both of which report a normal national range around $2,225–$8,133. We also compare against other established homeowner guides to check whether the headline range is directionally consistent.

Published national ranges are orientation only. Real contractor pricing may differ substantially because of scope, cause, local labor, soil, access, permits, engineering and materials.

Update policy

We review headline cost guidance when major source data changes and display a review date so visitors can see how current the planning information is.

Corrections
