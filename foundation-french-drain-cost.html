2026 DRAINAGE GUIDE

How much does a foundation French drain cost?

A French drain near a foundation can range from a relatively straightforward trench-and-pipe project to major excavation with difficult access and extensive restoration. The price depends less on the product name and more on the water path and installation conditions.

Main cost drivers

  • Drain length: more linear feet increase excavation, pipe, gravel and labor.
  • Depth: deeper work can require more excavation and safety planning.
  • Access: fences, patios, decks, landscaping and narrow side yards can slow equipment access.
  • Discharge: collected water needs a suitable outlet, and some systems may require a sump or pump.
  • Restoration: soil, plants, concrete or other finishes may need replacement after trenching.

French drain versus foundation waterproofing

A drain manages water; it does not automatically waterproof the foundation wall. If excavation is already required, some proposals may combine drainage with membrane or coating work. Compare that separately with our foundation waterproofing cost guide.

Make sure the contractor explains the water path

Ask where water is coming from, where the drain will intercept it and where it will discharge. A drain that has no reliable outlet or is placed without understanding site grading may not solve the actual problem.

For a broader overview of grading, drains, sump systems and other options, see our foundation drainage repair cost guide.

Questions to ask before hiring

  • How many linear feet of drain are included?
  • How deep and wide will the trench be?
  • What pipe, gravel and filter material are specified?
  • Where will the water discharge?
  • Are excavation, hauling and landscape restoration included?
  • Will permits or utility locating be required?

Interior and exterior systems are different

An exterior French drain should not be confused with an interior basement perimeter drain or drain-tile system. If the proposal is inside the basement, compare our basement drain tile cost guide instead.

This guide is for planning only. Drainage design should reflect the property's grading, soil, foundation and local requirements.
