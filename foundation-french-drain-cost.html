2026 DRAINAGE GUIDE

How much does a foundation French drain cost?

A French drain near a foundation can range from a relatively straightforward trench-and-pipe project to major excavation with difficult access and extensive restoration. The total is driven less by the product name than by the water path, trench conditions, discharge plan and what must be removed and rebuilt to install the system.

When comparing bids, focus on the scope. Two contractors can both write “French drain” while proposing very different trench depths, pipe layouts, gravel quantities, discharge points and restoration work.

Main cost drivers

  • Drain length: more linear feet increase excavation, pipe, gravel, fabric and labor.
  • Depth: deeper work may require more excavation, shoring or safety planning and creates more soil to move.
  • Access: fences, patios, decks, landscaping, utilities and narrow side yards can limit equipment and increase hand labor.
  • Soil and groundwater: heavy clay, rock, unstable soil or active water can make trenching slower and more complicated.
  • Discharge: collected water needs a reliable outlet. Some properties can use gravity while others may require a basin or pump.
  • Waterproofing add-ons: exterior membrane, coating, drainage board or wall repairs can be added when the foundation is already exposed.
  • Restoration: topsoil, sod, plants, pavers, concrete, asphalt or other finishes may need replacement after trenching.

What should be included in the estimate?

A useful quote should describe the project from collection to discharge. It should state the approximate drain length, trench depth, pipe type, aggregate or gravel, filter fabric, cleanouts if used, discharge location and whether a pump or basin is included. It should also explain excavation, hauling and restoration.

Do not compare only the bottom-line price. A lower quote may exclude soil removal, landscape repair, downspout work, pump equipment or waterproofing that another contractor included. Ask both contractors to define the same scope before comparing price.

French drain versus foundation waterproofing

A drain manages water; it does not automatically waterproof the foundation wall. Exterior waterproofing is intended to reduce water entry through the wall or joint, while drainage gives groundwater a controlled route away from the structure. Some projects need only drainage. Others combine both.

If excavation is already required, a contractor may propose cleaning the wall, repairing cracks, applying membrane or coating and adding drainage protection before backfilling. Compare that work separately with our foundation waterproofing cost guide so you know what is included in the drainage price.

Exterior French drain versus interior drain tile

An exterior foundation drain should not be confused with an interior basement perimeter system. Exterior work requires access around the house and can involve substantial excavation and landscape restoration. Interior drain tile is typically installed by opening the basement floor near the wall, collecting water below the slab and routing it to a sump system.

Neither approach is automatically better for every property. The right method depends on where the water is entering, the foundation type, grading, groundwater conditions and whether exterior excavation is practical. If the proposed system is inside the basement, compare our basement drain tile cost guide instead.

Make sure the contractor explains the full water path

A drainage system is only as useful as its route. Ask where the water originates, where the drain will intercept it, how the pipe will maintain flow and where the water will finally discharge. A trench can be installed correctly yet still underperform if the outlet is too high, frequently submerged or routed to an unsuitable location.

For a broader overview of grading, drains, sump systems and other options, see our foundation drainage repair cost guide. If surface runoff is the main problem, compare the foundation grading cost guide before assuming deep excavation is necessary.

When grading may be part of the problem

Water collecting next to a foundation can sometimes start above ground. Low spots, short downspout extensions, compacted soil or landscaping that slopes toward the house can send runoff directly to the foundation. A French drain may still be useful, but it should not become a substitute for correcting an obvious surface-water problem.

Ask whether the proposal includes final grading after the trench is backfilled. Poor restoration can recreate the same ponding condition the project was meant to solve.

How excavation changes the project

Excavation is often the largest difference between a simple drainage job and a major foundation project. A shallow landscape drain in open soil is not comparable with a drain installed beside a deep basement wall. Deeper excavation involves more labor, more spoil, more backfill and greater care around utilities and the structure.

If the contractor plans to expose the foundation wall deeply, compare our exterior foundation excavation cost guide. That helps separate the cost of digging and restoration from the drainage components themselves.

Questions to ask before hiring

  • How many linear feet of drain are included?
  • How deep and wide will the trench be?
  • What pipe, gravel and filter material are specified?
  • Will there be cleanouts or access points for future maintenance?
  • Where will collected water discharge?
  • Does the system rely on gravity or a pump?
  • Are excavation, hauling and backfill included?
  • Who is responsible for utility locating and permits?
  • Are landscaping, pavers, concrete or other restoration included?
  • Is any wall waterproofing, crack repair or drainage board included?
  • What happens if rock, buried utilities or unexpected foundation damage is found?
  • What workmanship or drainage warranty is provided?

How to compare two French drain bids

Put the estimates side by side and compare the same categories: drain length, trench depth, pipe size and type, aggregate, filter fabric, discharge route, pump equipment, waterproofing, excavation, hauling, restoration and warranty. If one proposal does not state an item, ask whether it is included rather than assuming.

A clear scope makes it easier to understand why one bid costs more and reduces the chance of discovering major exclusions after work starts.

When to get more than a drainage quote

If there are significant foundation cracks, movement, bowing walls or structural concerns, water management may be only one part of the repair. Consider an independent evaluation before committing to a large drainage project when the cause is unclear.
