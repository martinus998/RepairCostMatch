2026 ENGINEERING COST GUIDE

How much does a structural engineer cost for a foundation?

Current 2026 consumer guidance commonly places many independent structural-engineer foundation assessments around $400 to $700. A straightforward site visit may sit near the lower end, while larger homes, detailed reports, calculations, stamped plans, repair drawings or return visits can cost more.

The biggest pricing question is not simply “What is the engineer's hourly rate?” It is what level of work you are buying. A diagnostic visit, a written opinion and a full repair design are different scopes.

What the engineering fee may include

  • Site inspection: visual review, measurements and discussion of symptoms such as cracks, wall movement, uneven floors or settlement.
  • Written report: some engagements include a formal summary while others price documentation separately.
  • Level or movement measurements: documenting floor elevation, wall deflection or visible displacement may be part of the assessment.
  • Calculations or repair design: drawings, specifications or stamped plans can add cost beyond a basic diagnostic visit.
  • Follow-up: contractor questions, permit support, design revisions or a second visit may be separate line items.

Why homeowners pay for an independent opinion

Foundation contractors can be valuable for pricing and installing repairs, but they also sell the repair system. When a proposal is expensive or several contractors recommend different solutions, an independent engineer can help clarify the condition before you compare installation bids.

This can be especially useful if one contractor recommends multiple piers while another recommends drainage correction, if a basement wall is bowing, or if the house has several symptoms at once. For the broader decision, compare our foundation inspection cost guide. If you already have bids, use the foundation repair cost guide to compare scope rather than only totals.

Assessment versus repair design

A basic assessment may answer whether the observed movement appears significant and what category of repair deserves consideration. A repair design goes further by defining the proposed structural solution, dimensions, spacing, support details or other engineering requirements.

That difference matters when comparing prices. A low-cost visit that provides only verbal guidance should not be compared directly with a fee that includes calculations, drawings and stamped documents.

What changes the engineering price

Home size, foundation type, crawl-space access, number of problem areas, complexity of movement, travel, local professional rates and the level of documentation all affect the fee. A difficult crawl space can take more time than an open basement. A single crack may require less work than evaluating movement across several rooms, walls and support lines.

If the main issue is uneven floors or visible structural movement, our house leveling cost guide explains how the proposed repair can range from localized support adjustment to major lifting or underpinning.

When engineering involvement may be worth considering

  • A major stabilization or underpinning project is being proposed.
  • Several contractors disagree about the cause or repair method.
  • A basement wall shows significant inward movement or horizontal cracking.
  • Floors, doors and cracks suggest movement across more than one part of the house.
  • You want an assessment from someone who is not selling the repair installation.

For wall movement, see our bowing basement wall repair cost guide. For crack patterns, use the foundation crack guide. For settlement symptoms, see foundation settling signs.

Questions to ask before booking

  • Is the fee for inspection only, or does it include a written report?
  • Are measurements, calculations or drawings included?
  • Will the engineer identify likely repair categories or only document conditions?
  • Are stamped plans included if the contractor or permit office needs them?
  • Is a follow-up visit included after contractor work begins?
  • Will contractor questions or design revisions cost extra?
  • Are permit or plan-review services priced separately?

How to use the engineer's opinion when comparing bids

Once the condition is clearer, ask contractors to quote the same general repair scope whenever possible. If one bid includes five piers and another includes twelve, or one includes drainage correction while another does not, compare the assumptions before comparing the totals.
