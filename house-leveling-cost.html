2026 LEVELING COST GUIDE

How much does house leveling cost?

“House leveling” can describe several very different repairs, which is why online prices often look inconsistent. A settled concrete section may need slab lifting, a crawl-space floor may need support adjustment, and a structurally settling foundation may require piers, underpinning or controlled lifting.

For planning, 2026 public cost guides show localized concrete leveling commonly in the hundreds to low-thousands, while structural foundation leveling or lifting can rise into the several-thousand to tens-of-thousands range. The repair method matters far more than one national average.

First determine what “leveling” means in the proposal

One contractor may use the term for lifting a concrete slab. Another may be adjusting crawl-space posts or beams. A foundation specialist may be stabilizing settlement with piers and lifting only part of the structure. Those are different jobs with different risks, equipment and pricing.

Before comparing estimates, ask each contractor to identify what is out of level, what caused the movement, what part of the structure they plan to move and whether the goal is stabilization, partial correction or full lifting.

Common types of leveling work

  • Concrete or slab leveling: mudjacking or polyurethane foam may raise some sunken concrete sections when the underlying condition is suitable.
  • Crawl-space adjustment: posts, beams, shims or support pads may be corrected when floors are uneven because of conditions below the house.
  • Piering or underpinning: deeper supports may be installed when settlement is related to weak, moving or inadequate soil support.
  • Pier-and-beam correction: support points, beams or shims can be adjusted when a pier-and-beam structure is uneven.
  • Foundation raising: larger structural lifting projects can require synchronized jacking, temporary support and coordination around connected utilities and finishes.

Why estimates can differ dramatically

A contractor proposing to lift a slab is not pricing the same scope as one proposing ten piers under a settling foundation. Likewise, a crawl-space beam adjustment is not the same as lifting the load-bearing structure. When bids differ sharply, compare the measured problem and the intended repair before comparing totals.

If piers are involved, see our foundation repair cost per pier, helical pier cost and push pier cost guides. For broader stabilization methods, see foundation underpinning cost.

Stabilization versus lifting

These terms are easy to confuse. Stabilization is intended to stop or limit further movement. Lifting attempts to move part of the structure toward a different elevation. A project can include one or both, and the target correction may depend on the building condition, repair method and professional judgment.

Ask the contractor to state in writing whether the proposal includes stabilization only, lifting, an expected amount of correction, or no guaranteed lift. This makes it easier to compare proposals that otherwise use similar language.

Signs that leveling may be part of a larger foundation problem

Sloping floors, doors that stop fitting, separated trim, recurring cracks, visible settlement and movement across several rooms can justify a closer structural evaluation. A single low spot in a concrete slab is different from movement across the load-bearing structure.

If several symptoms appear together, review our foundation settling signs guide. If cracks are a major part of the problem, use our foundation crack guide. An independent inspection can also help clarify the scope before major work; see our foundation inspection cost guide.

What drives the cost

  • How much of the structure is affected.
  • Foundation type and access below or around the house.
  • Number and depth of piers or support points.
  • Soil conditions and whether deeper support is required.
  • Temporary shoring, jacking equipment and crew time.
  • Engineering, permits and required documentation.
  • Plumbing, electrical or mechanical systems that may be affected by movement.
  • Interior and exterior restoration after the structural work.

Questions to ask before approving a leveling job

  • What measurements show where the structure is out of level?
  • Are you stabilizing, lifting, or both?
  • What is causing the movement?
  • How many support points or piers are included?
  • What amount of correction is the proposal designed to achieve?
  • Are plumbing, electrical, engineering, permits and restoration included?
  • What happens if the structure does not move as expected?
  • What does the warranty cover after the work is complete?

How to compare two leveling bids

Put the proposals side by side and compare the measured areas, repair method, number of supports, lift target, drainage or soil work, engineering, permit responsibility and restoration. A cheaper proposal may simply include fewer support points or no lift. A more expensive proposal may include engineering, interior repairs or drainage corrections that the other bid excludes.
