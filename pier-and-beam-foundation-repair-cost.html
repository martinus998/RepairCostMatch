How much does pier and beam foundation repair cost?

Pier-and-beam repairs can involve several different systems beneath the home: piers or posts, beams, joists, shims, footings and moisture control. A quote should identify which component is actually failing instead of treating every uneven floor as the same problem.

Common repair scopes

  • Support adjustment: individual posts, piers or shims may need correction when support has shifted or settled.
  • Beam repair: damaged or undersized beams can require reinforcement or replacement.
  • Joist repair: sagging, split or moisture-damaged floor joists may need sistering or replacement.
  • Leveling: lifting and adjustment may be included when the structure has moved out of level.
  • Moisture correction: drainage, vapor control or other moisture work may be needed if deterioration is part of the problem.

Why two bids can be very different

One contractor may price only support adjustment while another includes beam replacement, joist work, drainage and restoration. Compare the written scope line by line. Ask what will be stabilized, what will be replaced and whether the goal is only to stop movement or also to improve floor level.

If supports are the main issue, see crawl space support post cost. For beam work, use our crawl space beam repair cost guide. For uneven floors, compare sagging floor repair cost.

Cost factors to document

Important drivers include the number of support points, crawl-space height, beam length, damaged joist count, soil conditions, moisture, temporary shoring, engineering, permits and how much finish work must be restored afterward.

Questions to ask contractors

  • Which pier, post, beam or joist is failing?
  • What caused the movement or deterioration?
  • Will the house be stabilized, leveled, or both?
  • How many support points are being added or repaired?
  • Are moisture correction, engineering, permits and cleanup included?
