How much does pier and beam foundation repair cost?

Pier-and-beam repairs can involve several different systems beneath the home: piers or posts, footings, beams, floor joists, shims and moisture control. A useful quote should identify which component is actually failing instead of treating every uneven floor as the same problem.

That distinction matters because a minor support adjustment is not the same project as replacing a damaged beam, repairing multiple joists and correcting a wet crawl space. Compare the repair method and quantity before comparing the total price.

Common pier-and-beam repair scopes

  • Pier or post adjustment: individual support points may need correction when they have shifted, settled or lost proper contact.
  • New or replacement supports: damaged, inadequate or missing supports may require new posts, piers or footings.
  • Beam repair: a girder or beam can require reinforcement or replacement when it is damaged, undersized or poorly supported.
  • Floor-joist repair: sagging, split or moisture-damaged joists may need sistering, reinforcement or replacement.
  • Leveling: controlled lifting or adjustment may be included when the structure has moved out of level.
  • Moisture correction: drainage, vapor control, leaks or humidity may need a separate repair if deterioration is connected with a wet crawl space.

Supports, beams and joists are different parts of the load path

A pier-and-beam floor system transfers load through joists to beams and then through posts, piers or other supports into the ground. A problem at one level can create symptoms somewhere else. For example, a sagging floor may come from a damaged joist, a deflected beam, a settled support or more than one of those conditions.

When comparing bids, ask the contractor to identify the failed component. For support-specific work, see our crawl space support post cost guide. For larger framing members, compare the crawl space beam repair cost guide. For joists, use the floor joist repair cost guide.

Why two pier-and-beam estimates can be very different

One contractor may price only support adjustment while another includes new footings, beam repair, joist reinforcement, leveling, drainage and restoration. The two totals are not comparable until the written scope is broken into the same categories.

Ask how many supports are being adjusted or replaced, how many feet of beam are included, how many joists are affected and whether moisture work is a separate line item.

Leveling and structural repair are not always the same thing

Leveling describes an outcome — improving the elevation or flatness of the structure. Structural repair describes the work needed to support the building safely. A contractor may stabilize a structure without returning every floor to perfectly level, especially when finishes, framing history or risk of damage limit how much correction is appropriate.

Ask whether the proposal is intended to stabilize, level or do both. If uneven flooring is the main symptom, compare our sagging floor repair cost guide and house leveling cost guide.

Access can change labor significantly

Crawl-space height, plumbing, HVAC ducts, electrical wiring, insulation and debris can all affect how easily workers can reach the support system. Low clearance may require more hand work and can make moving beams, posts and tools much slower.

Some repairs also require temporary shoring while existing supports or beams are changed. The estimate should state whether that support is included.

Moisture can turn a support problem into a larger repair

Persistent crawl-space moisture can contribute to wood rot, corrosion, mold and changing soil conditions. Structural repair alone may not address the cause if water continues entering or humidity stays high.

Use our crawl space moisture repair cost guide to separate drainage, vapor control, encapsulation and dehumidification from structural work. If wood deterioration is already present, compare the crawl space wood rot repair cost guide.

Soil and footing conditions matter

Piers and posts ultimately depend on the support below them. Settlement, soft soil, undersized footings or poor drainage can affect how well the system carries load. A proposal that adds or replaces supports should explain what those supports will bear on and why the chosen method fits the site.

If the repair involves deeper stabilization rather than ordinary crawl-space support, compare the broader foundation repair cost guide and foundation underpinning cost guide.

What a clear estimate should include

A detailed pier-and-beam estimate should identify the number and location of supports being adjusted, added or replaced; the beam and joist work; the planned amount of leveling; temporary shoring; moisture work; engineering; permits; cleanup and any finish restoration.

It should also state how additional hidden damage will be handled if deteriorated framing is discovered after work begins.

How to compare two contractor bids

Make a side-by-side list of support count, beam length, joist count, leveling goal, temporary shoring, moisture correction, engineering, permits and cleanup. If one proposal does not identify quantities, ask for clarification before treating it as equivalent to another bid.

A lower bid may simply repair fewer components. The written scope should explain why each repair is needed.

Questions to ask contractors

  • Which pier, post, beam or joist is failing?
  • What evidence shows that component is the cause?
  • What caused the movement or deterioration?
  • Will the house be stabilized, leveled, or both?
  • How many support points are being adjusted, added or replaced?
  • What footing or bearing condition will new supports use?
  • How many beams or joists are being repaired?
  • Will temporary shoring be required?
  • Is moisture correction included or separate?
  • Are engineering and permits required?
  • What cleanup and restoration are included?
  • How will additional hidden damage be priced?

When an independent evaluation may help

If floors are moving quickly, multiple structural components appear damaged, soil settlement is suspected or contractors recommend very different repair systems, an independent assessment can help define the problem before a major project is approved.

Frequently asked questions

Does every uneven floor mean the piers are failing?

No. Uneven floors can come from joists, beams, supports, settlement or a combination of issues. The failed component should be identified first.

Can a pier-and-beam home be leveled without replacing framing?

Sometimes support adjustment may be enough, but damaged beams or joists still require separate repair when deterioration is present.

Does encapsulation repair foundation supports?

No. Encapsulation manages crawl-space moisture. Structural supports and damaged framing require their own repair scope.

Why do bids vary so much?

Contractors may include different numbers of supports, different framing repairs, different leveling goals and different moisture or restoration work.
