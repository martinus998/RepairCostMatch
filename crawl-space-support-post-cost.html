2026 CRAWL SPACE SUPPORT GUIDE

How much does a crawl space support post cost?

A crawl-space support-post project can be simple when one localized support is being corrected, or much larger when several posts, footings, beams and floor-level issues are involved. The total depends less on the post itself and more on the structural scope around it.

Before comparing bids, make sure each contractor is solving the same problem: inadequate support, a failed post, a settled footing, beam damage or broader floor movement.

What may be included in the work

  • Adjustable or permanent support posts: installed to support a beam or girder at designated locations.
  • Concrete pads or footings: used to spread the load into suitable bearing material.
  • Beam reinforcement: needed when the member above the post is damaged, undersized or sagging.
  • Temporary shoring: used while the permanent support system is installed or adjusted.
  • Leveling: gradual lifting may be part of some scopes, while other projects aim only to stabilize current movement.
  • Finish or insulation work: access may require temporary removal of insulation or other materials.

Post count is only one cost driver

More posts generally mean more material and labor, but two projects with the same number of supports can still have very different totals. Footing size, crawl-space height, concrete access, beam condition and lifting requirements can all change the scope.

Ask the contractor to show the planned post locations and explain why each support is needed.

The footing or pad matters

A support post transfers load into whatever is beneath it. Placing a new post on loose soil, a thin slab or an inadequate pad may not create a reliable load path.

Ask what base or footing is included, how it is sized and whether excavation, concrete, curing time and cleanup are included in the estimate.

Why access changes the price

Tight crawl spaces make excavation, concrete work, material handling and post installation slower. Plumbing, ducts, wiring, wet soil and limited entry height can add labor even when only a few supports are involved.

If access is especially restricted, ask how materials and concrete will be moved into the work area and whether any utilities must be temporarily relocated.

Support posts do not automatically solve the cause

If a post settled because the footing was inadequate, replacing only the post may not solve the problem. If the beam above is damaged, new supports may not be enough by themselves. A useful proposal should describe the full load path from the floor framing through the beam, post and footing.

For beam-specific work, see crawl space beam repair cost. For broader support and leveling projects, compare crawl space foundation repair cost.

Stabilization versus lifting

A support-post project can be designed to stabilize the current floor position, or it can include controlled lifting. Those are different goals and can affect labor, monitoring and risk to finishes above.

Ask whether lifting is included, how much movement is expected and whether doors, drywall, flooring or plumbing could be affected during adjustment.

Beam condition can change the whole project

Adding a strong post below a damaged beam does not restore the beam itself. Rot, splitting, excessive deflection or poor connections may require reinforcement or replacement in addition to the new support.

Compare the crawl space wood rot repair cost guide when moisture damage is visible.

Moisture and soil conditions can affect support work

Wet soil, drainage problems and long-term crawl-space moisture can contribute to settlement and wood deterioration. Structural work may need a separate moisture-control plan so the new support system is not exposed to the same conditions.

Use crawl space moisture repair cost and crawl space drainage cost when water control is part of the project.

Questions to ask when comparing estimates

  • How many posts are being installed or replaced?
  • Where will each post be located?
  • What type and size of footing or pad is included?
  • Is the beam above the post structurally sound?
  • Is the scope stabilization only, or does it include lifting?
  • Will temporary shoring be required?
  • Is concrete excavation and cleanup included?
  • Are plumbing, wiring or duct relocations included?
  • Are engineering, permits and inspections included?
  • What warranty applies to the support system?

When sagging floors may point to a larger issue

If several rooms slope, doors stop fitting or the floor is noticeably bouncy, the problem may involve joists, beams, multiple posts or settlement. Our sagging floor repair cost guide explains how those scopes can overlap.

How to compare two support-post bids

Compare post count, locations, footing details, beam work, lifting goal, temporary shoring, access, moisture correction, engineering and restoration. A proposal listing only “install support posts” does not show enough detail for an equal comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Can a post sit directly on the soil?

Structural supports generally require an appropriate load-bearing base or footing rather than loose soil.

Does a new post automatically level the floor?

No. Some projects stabilize only. Controlled lifting is a separate goal that should be stated in the proposal.

What if the beam is damaged?

Beam reinforcement or replacement may be needed in addition to the support post.

Can moisture affect support posts?

Yes. Wet soil and crawl-space moisture can affect footings, framing and long-term durability.
