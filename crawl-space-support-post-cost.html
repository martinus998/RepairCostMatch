2026 CRAWL SPACE SUPPORT GUIDE

How much does a crawl space support post cost?

A crawl-space support-post project can be simple when one localized support is being corrected, or much larger when several posts, footings, beams and floor-level issues are involved. The total depends less on the post itself and more on the structural scope around it.

Before comparing bids, make sure each contractor is solving the same problem: inadequate support, a failed post, a settled footing, beam damage or broader floor movement.

What may be included in the work

  • Adjustable or permanent support posts: installed to support a beam or girder at designated locations.
  • Concrete pads or footings: used to spread the load into suitable bearing material.
  • Beam reinforcement: needed when the member above the post is damaged, undersized or sagging.
  • Temporary shoring: used while the permanent support system is installed or adjusted.
  • Leveling: gradual lifting may be part of some scopes, while other projects aim only to stabilize current movement.

Why access changes the price

Tight crawl spaces make excavation, concrete work, material handling and post installation slower. Plumbing, ducts, wiring, wet soil and limited entry height can add labor even when only a few supports are involved.

Support posts do not automatically solve the cause

If a post settled because the footing was inadequate, replacing only the post may not solve the problem. If the beam above is damaged, new supports may not be enough by themselves. A useful proposal should describe the full load path from the floor framing through the beam, post and footing.

For beam-specific work, see crawl space beam repair cost. For broader support and leveling projects, compare crawl space foundation repair cost.

Questions to ask when comparing estimates

  • How many posts are being installed or replaced?
  • What type and size of footing or pad is included?
  • Is the beam above the post structurally sound?
  • Is the scope stabilization only, or does it include lifting?
  • Will temporary shoring be required?
  • Are engineering, permits and cleanup included?

When sagging floors may point to a larger issue

If several rooms slope, doors stop fitting or the floor is noticeably bouncy, the problem may involve joists, beams, multiple posts or settlement. Our sagging floor repair cost guide explains how those scopes can overlap.

This guide is for planning only. It is not a structural inspection, engineering opinion or contractor quote.
