Vertical foundation crack: what the pattern can tell you

A vertical or near-vertical crack in a poured-concrete foundation can have several explanations. Some are related to shrinkage or normal movement, while others deserve a closer look because the crack is widening, displaced, repeatedly leaking or appearing with other movement symptoms.

What matters more than the direction alone

  • Width: a hairline crack and an open crack are not the same condition.
  • Displacement: check whether one side sits forward, backward, higher or lower than the other.
  • Change over time: dated photos can show whether the crack is stable or growing.
  • Water entry: repeated leakage can make repair necessary even when the crack is not structurally severe.
  • Other symptoms: sticking doors, sloping floors or additional cracks can change the context.

Water can turn a small crack into a bigger practical problem

When soil outside the wall becomes saturated, a vertical crack can allow water into the basement. In that situation, a contractor may discuss injection or sealing, but the broader drainage conditions still matter. Gutters, downspouts, grading and exterior water pressure may need attention if moisture keeps returning.

When monitoring can be useful

If a crack is small, stable and not leaking, a professional may recommend monitoring instead of major structural work. Photograph the crack with a ruler or reference scale and repeat the photo from the same position. Cosmetic patching by itself does not prove that movement has stopped.

Warning signs that justify a closer look

  • The crack widens over a short period.
  • One side of the crack is visibly displaced.
  • The crack repeatedly opens after previous repair.
  • Water enters during rain despite prior sealing.
  • Several movement symptoms appear together.

For other patterns, compare our foundation crack guide, horizontal crack guide and stair-step crack guide.

Questions to ask before approving repair

  • Is the goal sealing, structural stabilization or both?
  • What evidence shows whether the crack is active?
  • Is drainage contributing to the problem?
  • What repair material is being proposed and why?
  • What happens if the crack reopens or leaks again?
