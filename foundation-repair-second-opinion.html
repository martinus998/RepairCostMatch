SECOND OPINION GUIDE

When should you get a second opinion on foundation repair?

A second opinion is most useful when the repair is expensive, the diagnosis is unclear, or contractors recommend very different methods. The goal is to clarify the problem before comparing price.

Situations where another opinion can add value

  • Two contractors recommend different repair systems.
  • A large pier or underpinning project is recommended with little written explanation.
  • The proposal does not show measurements, locations or quantities.
  • The visible symptoms seem limited but the recommended scope is extensive.
  • You want an independent assessment before signing a major structural contract.

Contractor estimate versus independent engineer

A contractor can inspect conditions and price work it performs. An independent licensed structural engineer can evaluate structural movement and repair need without selling the repair. They answer different questions, so an engineer can be especially useful when diagnosis—not just price—is disputed.

Bring the same information to each reviewer

Share the written proposal, photos, crack locations, floor-elevation information if available, drainage history, plumbing findings and any previous repairs. Consistent information makes comparisons more meaningful.

Compare conclusions before prices

If two contractors disagree, identify exactly where they differ: cause, repair method, number of supports, drainage work, lifting versus stabilization, or restoration. Price comparison comes after the diagnosis and scope are clear.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to get a second opinion on foundation repair?

No. A major home repair is a significant decision, and another written opinion can help clarify scope and diagnosis.

Is a structural engineer the same as a foundation contractor?

No. A contractor typically diagnoses and sells repair work; an independent licensed engineer can evaluate structural conditions without performing the repair.

Can a second opinion lower the cost?

It may identify scope differences or optional work, but it may also confirm that the original scope is appropriate. The value is better information, not a guaranteed lower price.
