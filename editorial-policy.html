EDITORIAL POLICY

Useful information without pretending to know more than we do.

Our goal is to help homeowners prepare for a repair conversation with clearer expectations, not to replace a qualified inspection or manufacture certainty.

Source before claim

Headline cost guidance should be tied to identifiable sources and a review date. When data is broad or national, we say so rather than presenting it as a local quote.

Estimate ≠ quote

Planning bands are always separated from actual contractor pricing. We do not represent a web estimate as a guaranteed price.

No fake authority

We do not invent customer reviews, expert reviewers, contractor rankings, certifications or safety claims. Expert review will only be displayed when a real reviewer has actually reviewed the content.

Commercial transparency

If referrals, sponsored placements or partners generate revenue, that relationship should be disclosed clearly and should not be hidden inside educational content.

Corrections and updates

Broken sources, outdated cost data and material factual errors should be corrected promptly. Updated pages should carry a current review date where it helps readers judge freshness.

Safety language

When a symptom could indicate meaningful structural movement, flooding or utility risk, the site should encourage appropriate professional evaluation rather than pushing a conversion.

What we optimize for

ClarityPlain language that a homeowner can use before a sales call.
TransparencyVisible limitations, sources and commercial disclosures.
UsefulnessActionable questions, realistic ranges and clear next steps.
