Foundation leak repair cost in 2026

“Foundation leak” can describe several very different problems. Water may be entering through a wall crack, coming up at the slab edge, moving through a basement wall under hydrostatic pressure, or leaking from plumbing beneath a concrete slab. Those situations can require different trades and very different budgets.

Typical 2026 cost ranges

Angi's 2026 foundation inspection guide lists foundation leak repair at roughly $2,000 to $7,000 as a broad repair category. Its separate 2026 slab-leak cost guide reports approximately $630 to $4,400 for slab leak repair, depending heavily on the location of the leak, access and the amount of slab or finish restoration required.

General foundation repair is broader again: Angi's 2026 foundation repair guide reports an average planning range of about $2,225 to $8,133. These ranges overlap because “leak repair” can be a plumbing, waterproofing, drainage or structural project rather than one standardized repair.

Angi foundation inspection and repair cost data and Angi slab leak cost data provide current 2026 consumer ranges.

First identify what kind of leak you have

A contractor quote is only useful if the source is correctly identified. Before comparing prices, separate these common categories:

  • Wall or floor crack seepage: water entering through a localized concrete or masonry opening.
  • Exterior drainage problem: grading, downspouts, groundwater or failed perimeter drainage directing water toward the foundation.
  • Basement waterproofing problem: recurring moisture through walls, joints or the floor that may need interior or exterior water control.
  • Slab plumbing leak: a damaged water line beneath or within a concrete slab.
  • Structural foundation damage: movement, settlement, bowing or large cracking that requires stabilization in addition to water control.

Slab plumbing leaks can have different repair methods

When the water source is a pipe below a concrete slab, repair may involve direct access through the slab, pipe rerouting, pipe lining or tunneling, depending on the pipe location and condition. The plumbing repair cost is only part of the project if flooring, concrete, cabinets or other finishes must be removed and rebuilt.

Angi's current slab-leak guide emphasizes that access is a major cost driver. Leaks under finished floors or built-ins can require more demolition and restoration than easily accessible locations.

Waterproofing and drainage are separate scopes

If the foundation itself is allowing water entry, do not assume a plumbing repair will solve it. Exterior drainage, grading, French drains, waterproofing membranes, interior drain tile or a sump system may be part of the solution depending on where the water is coming from.

Use our foundation drainage repair cost, foundation grading cost, foundation French drain cost and foundation waterproofing cost guides to compare those scopes.

Cracks need context, not just filler

A small, stable crack that leaks can be very different from a crack associated with settlement or wall movement. Repairing the visible opening may stop localized seepage, but it may not address the reason the crack formed. If there are signs of movement, widening cracks, sticking doors, sloping floors or bowing walls, consider an independent structural evaluation before committing to a major repair.

What makes foundation leak repair more expensive?

  • Access: finished basements, cabinetry, flooring and deep exterior excavation increase labor.
  • Water source: plumbing, groundwater and drainage failures require different repair methods.
  • Structural damage: settlement or wall movement can turn a water-control job into a stabilization project.
  • Restoration: concrete, drywall, flooring, landscaping and finishes may need reconstruction after access.
  • Mold or rot: prolonged moisture may create separate remediation or wood-repair costs.
  • Permits and engineering: major structural or plumbing work can require additional professional and municipal costs.

Questions to ask when comparing bids

  • What evidence identifies the actual source of the water?
  • Is this a plumbing repair, waterproofing repair, drainage repair or structural repair?
  • What demolition and restoration are included?
  • Does the proposal include crack repair, drainage work or both?
  • Will exterior excavation be required?
  • Are mold, damaged finishes or structural repairs excluded?
  • What warranty applies, and what conditions can void it?
