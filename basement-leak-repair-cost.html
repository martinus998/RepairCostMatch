Basement leak repair cost in 2026

A basement leak is a symptom, not one standardized repair. Water can enter through a wall crack, the wall-floor joint, poor exterior drainage, a failed sump system, plumbing or a broader waterproofing failure. The cheapest quote is not useful if it addresses the wrong source.

Typical 2026 cost ranges

Angi's 2026 basement waterproofing cost data reports a typical professional range of about $2,461 to $8,202, with larger or more complex systems costing more. Its current basement-leak guidance notes that a small localized leak may cost around $250 to repair, while more extensive leaking areas can reach roughly $10,000 depending on the cause and scope.

These are broad planning numbers, not a quote. A crack injection, downspout correction, sump repair, interior drain system and exterior excavation are very different projects even though all can be described as “basement leak repair.”

Angi's 2026 basement waterproofing cost guide and leaky basement guide provide current consumer cost context.

Start by identifying where the water enters

Before comparing bids, note whether water appears after rain, near a plumbing fixture, at one crack, along the wall-floor joint or around a sump pit. Timing and location can narrow the likely repair category.

  • Wall crack seepage: may be a localized crack repair if the wall is otherwise stable.
  • Wall-floor joint water: can point to groundwater or drainage pressure around the foundation.
  • Water after heavy rain: may involve grading, gutters, downspouts, drainage or waterproofing.
  • Water near plumbing: may be a pipe, appliance or drain problem instead of foundation seepage.
  • Standing water near a sump: may involve pump capacity, power, discharge or drainage-system problems.

Exterior drainage can be part of the repair

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency notes that basement moisture can be caused by water flowing toward the foundation and recommends correcting outside water sources such as poor drainage and improper grading where appropriate.

That means an interior coating alone may not solve a leak caused by repeated exterior water pressure. Compare the actual drainage scope in each bid, including downspout routing, grading, exterior drains and excavation.

EPA basement moisture guidance explains why moisture problems should be corrected before remodeling or covering affected areas.

Crack repair and waterproofing are different scopes

A small stable crack can sometimes be repaired directly, but a basement with multiple leak paths or recurring water pressure may need a broader system. Interior drainage, sump pumps, vapor control, exterior membranes and excavation can each change the price substantially.

Do not ignore structural warning signs

If a leaking wall is also bowing, shifting or developing large or widening cracks, water control may be only one part of the project. Stabilization or structural repair can require a separate scope and budget.

For that situation, compare foundation repair cost and foundation inspection cost before accepting a waterproofing-only proposal.

Water damage and mold may be separate costs

Drywall, flooring, insulation, trim and stored materials can be damaged even after the leak source is fixed. EPA guidance emphasizes correcting the moisture source and drying wet materials promptly because damp conditions can support mold growth.

Cleanup, mold remediation and rebuild work are often separate from the waterproofing or foundation repair itself. Make sure a contractor's estimate clearly states what is included and excluded.

What makes a basement leak repair more expensive?

  • Multiple leak locations: a system-wide problem usually costs more than one isolated crack.
  • Exterior excavation: digging around the foundation increases labor and restoration work.
  • Finished basement surfaces: drywall, flooring and cabinetry may need removal and replacement.
  • Drainage work: interior drains, exterior drains or sump systems can add significant scope.
  • Structural movement: bowing or settlement can require stabilization in addition to water control.
  • Mold or damaged materials: cleanup and restoration may be separate projects.

Questions to ask when comparing bids

  • What evidence identifies the source of the water?
  • Is the proposal for a crack repair, drainage repair, sump system or waterproofing system?
  • Does the price include demolition and restoration?
  • Is exterior excavation required?
  • Are grading or downspout changes included?
  • Are mold, damaged drywall or flooring excluded?
  • What warranty applies to the exact repair being proposed?
  • What conditions would require additional structural work?
