2026 FLOOR REPAIR COST GUIDE

How much does sagging floor repair cost?

Sagging floors have one of the widest repair-cost ranges because the visible dip may come from the finish floor, subfloor, joists, beams, crawl-space supports or the foundation itself. Current 2026 Angi guidance places many sagging-floor repairs around $1,000 to $8,500, while major structural work can exceed that range.

What the repair may actually involve

  • Surface or subfloor repair: localized damage may stay toward the lower end when framing is sound.
  • Joist repair: sistering, reinforcing or replacing damaged joists can become a multi-thousand-dollar project. See our floor joist repair cost guide for this specific scope.
  • Beam or girder repair: damaged load-bearing members can require temporary shoring and more labor.
  • Posts and supports: failed or settling supports in a crawl space may need replacement or adjustment.
  • Foundation correction: settlement or structural movement can move the project into foundation-repair pricing.

Why the cause matters more than the dip

A contractor should be able to explain what is carrying the load and why that component is moving. Replacing flooring over a failing joist does not solve the structural cause, while installing structural supports may be unnecessary if the problem is only a damaged subfloor.

Cost factors that can change the bid

Access, floor area, number of damaged joists, beam length, moisture, pest damage, temporary shoring, finished ceilings below the repair and foundation movement can all affect cost. Tight crawl spaces and hidden deterioration often increase labor because the real scope becomes clear only after inspection.

If the problem appears to start below the floor framing, compare our crawl space foundation repair cost guide. If a beam is the main issue, see crawl space beam repair cost.

Questions to ask before approving work

  • Which structural member is causing the sag?
  • Is the plan to stabilize, level, replace damaged material, or all three?
  • Will floor finishes or ceilings need to be removed?
  • Is moisture or pest damage part of the cause?
  • Are engineering, permits, shoring and cleanup included?

2026 source notes

Angi's 2026 sagging-floor guide lists many projects around $1,000 to $8,500 and notes that joist or post damage can cost substantially more. HomeAdvisor also emphasizes that structural joist repairs are far more expensive than simple floor-surface work.
