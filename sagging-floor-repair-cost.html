2026 FLOOR REPAIR COST GUIDE

How much does sagging floor repair cost?

Sagging floors have one of the widest repair-cost ranges because the visible dip may come from the finish floor, subfloor, joists, beams, crawl-space supports or the foundation itself. The right repair depends on the failing component, not just how noticeable the sag looks from above.

The most useful way to compare bids is to separate diagnosis, structural repair, leveling, finish restoration and moisture correction instead of treating “fix sagging floor” as one line item.

What the repair may actually involve

  • Surface or subfloor repair: localized damage may be limited to nonstructural flooring layers when framing is sound.
  • Joist repair: sistering, reinforcing or replacing damaged joists can become a substantial structural project. See our floor joist repair cost guide.
  • Beam or girder repair: damaged load-bearing members can require temporary shoring and staged replacement. Compare crawl space beam repair cost.
  • Posts and supports: failed or settling supports may need replacement, new footings or adjustment. See crawl space support post cost.
  • Foundation correction: settlement or structural movement can move the project into foundation-repair pricing.

Why the cause matters more than the dip

A contractor should be able to explain what is carrying the load and why that component is moving. Replacing flooring over a failing joist does not solve the structural cause, while installing structural supports may be unnecessary if the problem is only a damaged subfloor.

Ask each contractor to identify the exact failing component and show how the proposed repair reconnects the load path.

Joists, beams and posts can fail together

A floor can sag because one joist is damaged, because a beam has deflected, because a support post settled or because several parts of the system are affected at once. That is why two estimates can look completely different even when both contractors are looking at the same room.

If the problem appears to start below the floor framing, compare our crawl space foundation repair cost guide.

Leveling is not the same as structural repair

Some proposals include gradual lifting or leveling after the structural problem is stabilized. Others focus only on preventing further movement. Those are different goals and should be described separately.

Ask how much correction is expected, how quickly the floor will be moved and what finishes, plumbing or framing could be affected during lifting.

Moisture can be part of the cause

Long-term crawl-space moisture can weaken joists, beams and sill areas. If rot is present, structural repair without moisture control can expose replacement materials to the same conditions.

Review crawl space moisture repair cost, crawl space wood rot repair cost and crawl space drainage cost when moisture is part of the scope.

Cost factors that can change the bid

Access, floor area, number of damaged joists, beam length, moisture, pest damage, temporary shoring, finished ceilings below the repair and foundation movement can all affect cost. Tight crawl spaces and hidden deterioration often increase labor because the real scope becomes clear only after inspection.

Plumbing, ductwork and wiring can also complicate sistering or beam replacement if they run through the framing that needs repair.

Finished areas can add restoration cost

Flooring, ceilings, drywall, trim or cabinets may need to be removed and replaced to access structural members. Ask whether those finish repairs are part of the quoted total or left to another contractor.

A lower structural bid can become a higher overall project if finish restoration is excluded.

Questions to ask before approving work

  • Which structural member is causing the sag?
  • Is the plan to stabilize, level, replace damaged material, or all three?
  • How much floor movement was measured?
  • Will temporary shoring be required?
  • Will floor finishes or ceilings need to be removed?
  • Is moisture or pest damage part of the cause?
  • Are beams, posts or footings included?
  • Are engineering, permits, cleanup and restoration included?
  • What happens if hidden deterioration is found?

How to compare two sagging-floor bids

Compare the diagnosed cause, joist count, beam work, support posts, lifting goal, temporary shoring, finish removal, moisture correction and restoration. Two totals are not directly comparable if one includes only framing reinforcement and the other includes leveling and finish work.

Frequently asked questions

Do sagging floors always mean foundation damage?

No. Joists, beams, supports, moisture damage and subfloor issues can create similar symptoms.

Can a floor be leveled without repairing the cause?

Leveling alone does not correct a failed structural component. The underlying support problem should be identified first.

Can moisture cause sagging floors?

Yes. Persistent moisture can contribute to wood decay in joists, beams and sill areas.

Why do contractor proposals differ so much?

They may be addressing different components, repair goals, support locations or restoration scopes.

This guide is for planning only and cannot determine whether a floor is structurally safe. Significant movement requires an appropriate on-site evaluation.
