2026 COST GUIDE

How much does foundation repair cost?

For U.S. homeowners, many 2026 consumer cost guides place typical foundation repair in roughly the $2,200 to $8,200 range, with national averages around the low-$5,000s. That figure is useful for planning, but it can be misleading if you do not know what kind of repair is actually needed.

A small non-structural crack may cost far less than the national average. A home that needs multiple piers, extensive excavation, wall stabilization, drainage correction or structural reconstruction can cost much more.

Why foundation repair prices vary so much

Foundation repair is not one service. Contractors may be solving water intrusion, soil movement, settlement, wall pressure, failed supports or cracking that is mostly cosmetic. Price depends on the repair method, number of support points, access around the house, local soil, permits, engineering, labor rates and whether related drainage or plumbing work is required.

Common project categories

  • Localized crack repair: often the lower-cost end when the crack is stable and the job does not require structural stabilization.
  • Water and drainage correction: may include grading, drains, sump systems, sealing or exterior waterproofing.
  • Settlement stabilization: can involve helical piers, push piers, underpinning or other support systems and is commonly priced by the number and type of supports.
  • Bowing wall repair: may involve anchors, straps, braces, drainage corrections or reconstruction depending on movement and access.
  • Major structural correction: extensive movement, excavation or reconstruction can push the project well above national averages.

If your main symptom is an uneven or settling structure, compare our house leveling cost guide. If the repair centers on a crack, use the foundation crack repair cost guide to separate simple sealing from structural work.

If you already know your foundation type, see our dedicated slab foundation repair cost guide or crawl space foundation repair cost guide for a narrower planning range.

How pier count can change the total

When settlement repair requires piers or underpinning, the number of support points can become one of the biggest cost drivers. The price is not simply “cost per pier × number of piers,” because mobilization, engineering, depth, soil and access also matter. See our foundation repair cost per pier guide for a more focused planning breakdown.

Signs that deserve professional evaluation

Not every crack means a failing foundation. However, widening cracks, stair-step cracking in masonry, horizontal basement wall cracks, doors that suddenly stop fitting, visibly sloping floors, separated trim or repeated cracking after prior repair can justify a closer look. Rapid change or visible structural movement should not be evaluated from an online cost guide alone.

What to compare between estimates

Do not compare only the final dollar amount. Ask whether each contractor is proposing the same repair method, number of piers or anchors, drainage scope, warranty terms, engineering involvement, permit responsibility and restoration work. Two bids can look very different because they are solving different versions of the problem.

How to reduce surprise costs

Document cracks with dated photos, ask for the proposed repair method in writing, verify whether engineering or permits are included, and ask what happens if the contractor discovers additional damage after work starts. If a contractor recommends a major stabilization project, a separate structural engineering opinion may be useful in some situations.
