2026 COST GUIDE

How much does foundation repair cost?

For U.S. homeowners, many 2026 consumer cost guides place typical foundation repair in roughly the $2,200 to $8,200 range, with national averages around the low-$5,000s. That is a useful planning band, but it becomes meaningful only after you know what problem the contractor is trying to solve.

A small stable crack may cost far less than the national average. A home that needs multiple piers, extensive excavation, wall stabilization, drainage correction or structural reconstruction can cost much more. The repair method matters more than one headline average.

Start with the problem, not the price

Foundation repair is not one service. Contractors may be solving settlement, lateral wall pressure, water intrusion, failed supports, poor drainage or cracking that is mostly cosmetic. Two estimates can differ dramatically because the contractors are proposing different scopes rather than charging different prices for the same work.

If you do not yet know what the visible symptom means, use our repair guide hub to start with settlement, cracks, water, crawl-space problems or bowing walls before comparing methods.

Common foundation repair categories

  • Localized crack repair: often the lower-cost end when the crack is stable and the job does not require structural stabilization. See our foundation crack repair cost guide.
  • Water and drainage correction: may include grading, drains, sump systems, sealing or exterior waterproofing. Compare our foundation drainage repair cost guide.
  • Settlement stabilization: can involve helical piers, push piers, underpinning or other support systems. See the underpinning guide, helical pier cost guide and push pier cost guide.
  • Bowing wall stabilization: may involve anchors, carbon fiber, steel beams, tiebacks, drainage correction or more invasive wall work depending on movement and access.
  • Major structural correction: extensive movement, excavation, rebuilding or combined structural and water-control work can push the project well above broad national averages.

Foundation type changes the repair

A slab, crawl-space foundation and pier-and-beam system do not fail or get repaired in exactly the same way. If you know the foundation type, use the dedicated slab foundation repair cost guide, crawl-space foundation repair guide or pier-and-beam repair guide.

If the main symptom is sloping or uneven floors, our house leveling cost guide helps separate leveling from broader stabilization work.

How pier count changes the total

When settlement repair requires piers or underpinning, the number of support points can become one of the biggest cost drivers. The final price is not simply “cost per pier × number of piers,” because mobilization, depth, soil, engineering, access and the amount of lifting or stabilization can change the scope.

Use our foundation repair cost per pier guide when you want to understand how support count affects an estimate.

Drainage work can be part of the structural solution

Repairing movement without addressing water or soil conditions can leave part of the underlying problem unresolved. Depending on the property, a proposal may also include grading, drainage, exterior excavation or waterproofing.

Compare these scopes separately with our foundation grading, French drain, foundation waterproofing and exterior excavation guides.

When an independent inspection may help

Not every crack needs an engineer, but an independent assessment can be useful when movement appears significant, several contractors recommend very different repairs, a large stabilization project is proposed, or you want a diagnosis that is separate from the company selling the repair.

Compare our foundation inspection cost guide and structural engineer foundation cost guide if you are considering a separate evaluation.

Signs that deserve closer evaluation

Not every crack means a failing foundation. However, widening cracks, stair-step cracking in masonry, horizontal basement wall cracks, doors that suddenly stop fitting, visibly sloping floors, separated trim or repeated cracking after prior repair can justify a closer look. Rapid change or visible structural movement should not be evaluated from an online cost guide alone.

For symptom-first guidance, start with foundation settling signs or the foundation crack guide.

How to compare contractor estimates

Do not compare only the final dollar amount. Ask whether each contractor is proposing the same repair method, quantity, support locations, drainage scope, warranty terms, engineering involvement, permit responsibility and restoration work.

  • What exactly is failing?
  • What repair method is being proposed, and why?
  • How many piers, anchors or support points are included?
  • Is lifting planned, or only stabilization?
  • Are engineering and permits included?
  • Is drainage correction part of the repair?
  • What conditions could increase the price after work begins?
  • What does the warranty actually cover?

How to reduce surprise costs

Document cracks with dated photos, ask for the repair method and quantities in writing, verify whether permits and engineering are included, and ask how hidden damage or difficult access would be priced. If excavation or interior restoration is involved, confirm whether cleanup, landscaping, drywall or flooring replacement is included.
