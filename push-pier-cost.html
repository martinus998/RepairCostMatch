2026 PUSH PIER COST GUIDE

How much do push piers cost?

Push piers, also called steel piers, are one method used to stabilize an existing foundation that has settled. Pricing is often discussed per pier, but the final project total depends on much more than the unit price. Pier count, depth, footing condition, access, engineering, excavation and whether a controlled lift is attempted can all change the scope.

Current 2026 consumer cost guides commonly place steel push piers at around $1,500 to $2,000 per pier, while broader underpinning estimates often fall in the $1,000 to $3,000 per-pier range. A multi-pier foundation project can therefore reach five figures quickly.

2026 push pier cost benchmarks

Angi's 2026 foundation-repair guide lists steel push piers at about $1,500 each. Its 2026 underpinning guide uses roughly $2,000 per push pier as a planning figure and notes that underpinning projects can range much higher depending on the number of support points and the overall repair.

Those figures are best used as orientation rather than a quote. Two homes can use the same pier system and have very different totals because one needs more piers, deeper installation, difficult access or more extensive repair around the footing.

What usually makes up a push pier quote?

A complete proposal may include local excavation at each support location, steel brackets, pier sections, hydraulic installation, load transfer, elevation measurements, backfill and cleanup. Engineering, permits, drainage work, concrete or landscape restoration may be separate.

Ask the contractor to identify every major line item instead of showing only one “per pier” number. A lower unit price is not necessarily a lower total if excavation, engineering or restoration are later added as extras.

Why pier count changes the project total

Pier count is one of the biggest cost drivers. A proposal for six push piers and a proposal for twelve are fundamentally different repairs even if their unit prices look close. More piers mean more excavation points, more steel, more brackets, more installation time and more restoration.

Ask how the contractor selected each pier location. The proposal should connect the pier layout to the area of settlement, structure and repair design. For a broader explanation of unit pricing, see our foundation repair cost per pier guide.

Stabilization versus lifting

One important question is whether the goal is to stabilize the foundation where it is or attempt to recover some lost elevation. Those are not the same scope. A proposal may include only support and load transfer, while another may include a controlled lift attempt with additional monitoring and risk discussion.

Ask the contractor to state the repair objective in writing. A promise to “level the house” should not be assumed from a basic pier price.

Depth, soil and installation criteria

The final depth can affect material and labor because additional pier sections may be needed. The contractor or engineer should also explain the installation criterion being used, such as required resistance or other project-specific acceptance criteria.

Depth alone should not be treated as a guarantee of performance. The correct design depends on the foundation, soil conditions, structural loads and the system being used.

Push piers versus helical piers

Push piers and helical piers can both be used for foundation support, but they work differently. Push piers rely on the structure as reaction weight during installation. Helical piers use screw-like helical plates and are advanced into the soil based on design and installation criteria.

Neither system is automatically superior for every property. The appropriate choice depends on the structure, footing, soil, access and repair design. If a contractor proposes the other system, compare our helical pier cost guide before comparing totals.

Access and excavation can raise the cost

Even though each pier is installed at a localized point, access still matters. Decks, patios, porches, landscaping, narrow side yards and interior foundation locations can make excavation or equipment placement more difficult. Restoration may also be more expensive when hardscape has to be removed and rebuilt.

If the project requires larger exterior digging beyond the individual pier locations, compare our exterior foundation excavation cost guide.

Engineering, permits and measurements

Some projects include structural engineering or permit work in the proposal; others do not. Ask whether design documents, pre-repair elevation measurements, post-installation measurements and permit fees are included.

If the cause of settlement or the repair plan is unclear, an independent evaluation can be useful before approving a large underpinning scope. See our structural engineer foundation cost guide.

Warranty terms matter

Foundation-repair warranties can sound similar while covering very different things. Ask whether the warranty covers the installed pier, a defined area of the structure, labor, materials or future adjustment. Also ask whether there are transfer requirements if the property is sold and whether periodic inspections are required.

Do not compare warranty length alone. A long headline term is only useful if you understand the actual coverage and exclusions.

Common exclusions to check

  • Structural engineering or design
  • Building permits
  • Additional pier sections if deeper installation is required
  • Footing repair or modification
  • Concrete, patio or landscaping restoration
  • Interior finish repair after movement
  • Drainage correction around the foundation
  • Elevation monitoring after installation

Questions to ask before approving push piers

  • How many piers are included and why were those locations selected?
  • What is the expected installation criterion?
  • Is the project stabilization only, or is lifting part of the scope?
  • Are engineering and permits included?
  • What happens if additional depth or pier sections are required?
  • Are excavation, brackets, backfill and restoration included?
  • Is footing repair included if damage is found?
  • What measurements will be taken before and after installation?
  • What exactly does the warranty cover?
  • Does the warranty transfer to a future owner?

How to compare two push pier estimates

Compare the complete scope rather than only the per-pier price. Put the bids side by side and check pier count, installation criteria, engineering, excavation, lifting objective, restoration, monitoring and warranty. A $1,600 unit price with major exclusions may be more expensive than a $2,000 unit price that includes more of the finished project.
