How much does foundation crack repair cost?

Foundation crack repair is not one standardized service. A stable poured-concrete crack that leaks can require a very different scope from a horizontal crack associated with wall movement or a stair-step crack associated with settlement.

Before comparing cost, identify what the contractor believes the crack represents: surface shrinkage, water entry, movement in the wall or broader foundation settlement.

Start with the crack pattern

Crack direction, location, displacement and change over time provide context. A narrow vertical crack in poured concrete is different from a horizontal crack across a basement wall or a stair-step pattern through masonry joints.

Use our foundation crack guide, vertical crack guide, stair-step crack guide and horizontal crack guide to organize the visible pattern before discussing repair.

Match the visible problem to the repair-cost path

The crack itself may be the whole project, or it may be one symptom of a larger structural or water-control problem. Choosing the right comparison page first helps prevent misleading price comparisons.

  • If the crack is stable and the main problem is leakage, compare crack injection cost and broader foundation waterproofing cost.
  • If the crack follows settlement or the house shows movement, review foundation repair cost, underpinning cost and cost per pier.
  • If a basement wall is moving inward, use the bowing basement wall repair cost guide rather than comparing the job with a simple crack seal.
  • If contractors disagree about the cause or propose major structural work, compare the scope of an independent structural engineer evaluation.
  • For the full set of related repair topics, use the RepairCostMatch repair guide hub.

Non-structural crack repair

Some stable cracks may be treated with injection, sealing or patching. The goal may be to reduce water entry, bond the crack or protect the concrete rather than correct structural movement.

If injection is specifically proposed, compare the basement wall crack injection cost guide.

Structural crack repair is a different project

If the crack is connected to settlement, wall bowing, displacement or continuing movement, sealing the visible line alone may not address the underlying condition. Structural work can involve reinforcement, anchors, beams, tiebacks, piers, underpinning or other stabilization.

For larger movement-related projects, compare foundation underpinning cost, foundation repair cost per pier and bowing basement wall repair cost.

Water leakage can add a separate scope

A crack can be structurally stable and still leak, or it can be part of a broader drainage problem. Ask whether the proposed crack treatment is intended to stop leakage and whether exterior water conditions need separate correction.

If water appears after storms, use water in basement after rain. For broader water management, compare foundation drainage repair cost and foundation waterproofing cost.

What changes the repair scope

  • Crack direction: vertical, diagonal, stair-step and horizontal patterns can point to different conditions.
  • Width and displacement: visible offset across a crack can matter more than width alone.
  • Movement over time: an actively changing crack requires a different conversation from a stable old crack.
  • Water intrusion: leakage may add sealing, drainage or waterproofing work.
  • Foundation material: poured concrete, block and masonry repairs use different methods.
  • Access: finished basements or exterior excavation can add demolition and restoration.
  • Underlying settlement: the project can expand into structural stabilization when the crack is only one symptom.

Document the crack before repair

Take dated photos and note whether nearby doors, windows or floors have changed. If the crack leaks, document the weather conditions and where water appears.

This record does not diagnose the problem, but it can help an inspector, engineer or contractor understand whether the condition appears stable or changing.

What a detailed crack-repair estimate should show

  • Crack location and approximate length.
  • Foundation material.
  • Whether the contractor considers the crack structural or non-structural.
  • Repair method and preparation.
  • Whether water sealing is included.
  • Any structural stabilization work.
  • Drainage or waterproofing scope.
  • Finish removal and restoration.
  • Warranty and exclusions.

Questions to ask a contractor

  • What do you believe caused the crack?
  • Do you consider it structural or non-structural, and why?
  • Is there displacement across the crack?
  • Does the crack appear active?
  • What repair method are you proposing?
  • Does the price include water-control work?
  • Could settlement or wall movement require a separate structural repair?
  • Would an independent structural opinion be useful before a major repair?

When an independent opinion can help

If contractors disagree sharply about whether the crack is structural, if a major underpinning or wall-stabilization project is recommended, or if multiple movement symptoms appear together, an independent engineer may help clarify the repair objective.

Compare our structural engineer foundation cost guide for that scope.

Frequently asked questions

Is every foundation crack structural?

No. The crack pattern, displacement, location and movement over time matter.

Is injection enough for a moving crack?

Not necessarily. Injection can treat the crack itself, but active structural movement may require a separate stabilization solution.

Should leaking cracks be evaluated for drainage?

Yes. Exterior water conditions can contribute to recurring leakage.

When should I seek an independent structural opinion?

It can be useful when movement is progressing, several structural symptoms appear together or major repair proposals differ significantly.

This page is educational and cannot determine whether a specific crack is structural. Rapid movement or visible instability should be evaluated by an appropriate qualified professional.
