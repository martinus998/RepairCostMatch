How much does basement waterproofing cost?

Basement waterproofing has one of the widest price ranges in home repair because “waterproofing” can mean anything from sealing a small leak to excavating around the foundation and installing a full drainage and membrane system.

Current 2026 consumer guides show very different averages depending on the work included. Angi lists many professional basement waterproofing projects around $2,461–$8,202, while larger full-system projects can run substantially higher. The useful planning number depends on the water source, access and the method used.

Start with where the water is coming from

The cheapest waterproofing system is not necessarily the right system. Water can enter through wall cracks, the wall-floor joint, porous masonry, window wells, plumbing, groundwater pressure or poor exterior drainage. Condensation can also look like leakage even though the repair is different.

If water appears mainly during or after storms, start with our water in basement after rain guide before comparing bids.

Common basement waterproofing methods

  • Crack sealing or injection: useful when water enters through a localized crack and the wall is otherwise stable.
  • Interior drainage: drain tile or channel systems collect water at the perimeter and route it to a sump. Compare our basement drain tile cost guide.
  • Sump pump systems: include the pit, pump, discharge and sometimes backup power. See our sump pump installation cost guide.
  • Exterior waterproofing: excavation, membranes and exterior drainage try to stop water before it enters the structure. This is generally more labor-intensive because soil, landscaping and exterior finishes may need to be removed and restored.
  • Grading and surface-water correction: gutters, downspouts and soil slope can be part of the solution when water is being directed toward the foundation.

Interior vs. exterior waterproofing

Interior systems manage water after it reaches the foundation perimeter. Exterior systems try to prevent water from reaching the wall in the first place. Neither method is automatically “better” in every house. The right choice depends on where water is entering, exterior access, foundation material, drainage and whether structural movement is also present.

When comparing proposals, ask each contractor to explain the water path they believe they are solving. If two contractors identify different causes, their prices may not be directly comparable.

What drives basement waterproofing cost

  • Basement size and perimeter length.
  • Interior versus exterior access.
  • Foundation material and depth below grade.
  • Excavation difficulty and restoration work.
  • Drainage layout and discharge route.
  • Sump-pump and backup-power requirements.
  • Finished walls, flooring or built-ins that must be removed.
  • Existing mold, damaged materials or structural problems.

If you are comparing projects by area, see our basement waterproofing cost per square foot guide, but remember that system type and access can matter more than square footage alone.

Waterproofing and structural repair are not the same thing

Water control may be only one part of the project. Horizontal cracks, inward wall movement or significant displacement can indicate a structural issue that should be evaluated separately from the waterproofing system.

If you see those symptoms, read our horizontal foundation crack guide, bowing basement wall guide and bowing wall repair cost guide.

Questions to ask a waterproofing contractor

  • Where do you believe the water is entering?
  • What evidence supports that diagnosis?
  • Why are you recommending interior, exterior or combined work?
  • Is drain tile included, and where will the water discharge?
  • Is a sump pump included? What about backup power?
  • What happens to finished walls, flooring or landscaping?
  • Are crack repairs, mold cleanup or structural work included?
  • What maintenance is required after installation?
  • What exactly does the warranty cover?

How to compare two waterproofing bids

Put the proposals side by side and compare method, total perimeter treated, drain length, pump specifications, discharge routing, wall treatment, exterior excavation, restoration and warranty. A lower bid may simply include less work.

Ask contractors to separate optional upgrades from required work. That makes it easier to see whether you are comparing two solutions to the same problem.
