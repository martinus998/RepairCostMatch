FOUNDATION COST GUIDE

How much does foundation repair cost per pier?

Homeowners often hear foundation repair priced “per pier,” but that number can be misleading when it is taken out of context. A pier is only one part of the repair system. The total project can also include engineering, excavation, mobilization, brackets, lifting, concrete work, permits, drainage corrections and restoration after the structural work is complete.

Why contractors use per-pier pricing

Settlement repairs commonly use push piers, helical piers or another underpinning method at several points around the affected section of the foundation. Because each support point requires materials and labor, the number of piers is a useful part of the estimate. However, two projects with the same pier count can still have very different totals.

What changes the cost of each pier

  • Pier type: push piers and helical piers use different installation methods and equipment.
  • Required depth: difficult soil or deeper competent bearing material can increase labor and material use.
  • Access: decks, landscaping, tight side yards and interior slabs can make installation harder.
  • Excavation and restoration: exposing the footing and restoring soil, concrete or landscaping can add cost.
  • Engineering and permits: some projects require professional design, inspections or local permits.
  • Lift expectations: stabilizing movement is not always the same as lifting the structure back toward its original position.

Why the cheapest per-pier quote may not be the cheapest project

A lower unit price can exclude work that another contractor includes. One bid may include engineering, concrete repair and warranty coverage while another lists those separately. Compare the entire scope, not just the number printed beside “pier.”

Questions to ask when a contractor recommends piers

  • What movement or elevation data supports the recommendation?
  • Why is this pier type appropriate for the soil and structure?
  • How many piers are included, and where will they be installed?
  • What depth or load criteria determine when each pier is complete?
  • Are engineering, permits, excavation and restoration included?
  • Is the goal stabilization, lift, or both?
  • What does the warranty cover and who services it later?

Use the pier count as one planning input

If a proposal calls for six piers and another calls for twelve, that does not automatically mean the second contractor is overcharging. The contractors may be addressing different sections of the structure or using different spacing assumptions. Ask each contractor to show the affected area and explain why the proposed layout is necessary.

When an independent opinion can help

If a major underpinning project is recommended and the scope is expensive or significantly different from another proposal, an independent structural engineer may help clarify the cause, movement and repair objective.
