Foundation repair cost per pier: calculator & guide

Homeowners often hear foundation repair priced “per pier,” but that number can be misleading when it is taken out of context. A pier is one support point inside a larger repair system. The total project may also include engineering, excavation, mobilization, brackets, lifting, concrete work, permits, drainage corrections and restoration.

The right comparison is not simply price per pier. It is pier type + pier count + repair objective + everything included around those support points.

Why contractors use per-pier pricing

Settlement repairs commonly use push piers, helical piers or another underpinning method at several points around the affected section of the foundation. Because each support point requires materials and labor, pier count is a useful estimate component.

However, two projects with the same pier count can still have very different totals because access, depth, engineering, excavation and restoration are project-specific.

What changes the cost of each pier

  • Pier type: push piers and helical piers use different installation methods and equipment.
  • Required depth or resistance: soil conditions and design criteria can affect how much material and installation effort are required.
  • Access: decks, landscaping, narrow side yards, porches and interior slabs can complicate installation.
  • Excavation: footing access may require digging, concrete removal or interior slab openings.
  • Bracket and connection details: the foundation connection is part of the structural system, not just the pier shaft.
  • Lift expectations: stabilization only is different from controlled elevation recovery.

Push piers and helical piers are not interchangeable line items

Push piers use the structure during installation and are commonly proposed for certain settlement conditions. Helical piers are advanced into soil using rotational resistance and can be used in different structural and soil situations.

For system-specific scope, compare our push pier cost guide and helical pier cost guide. The appropriate method should come from the repair design rather than whichever unit price looks lower.

Why pier count can differ between contractors

One contractor may recommend support only beneath the visibly affected section. Another may extend the repair farther based on elevation readings, cracking, structural layout or its own spacing assumptions.

If one proposal calls for six piers and another calls for twelve, do not compare totals until both contractors show the affected foundation length and proposed support locations. Ask what evidence supports the layout.

Project-level costs make the unit price incomplete

Some costs do not scale directly with each pier. Mobilization, engineering, permits, plumbing evaluation, concrete saw-cutting, landscaping restoration and site cleanup may apply to the entire project.

That is why multiplying a website “cost per pier” number by a contractor's pier count can create a poor estimate. The unit price is only one piece of the complete scope.

Stabilization versus lifting

A proposal may be intended to stop additional settlement without promising that the structure will return to its original elevation. Another proposal may include a controlled attempt to improve elevation where conditions allow.

Ask every contractor to state the objective in writing. If lifting is proposed, ask what realistic result is expected and what risks or limitations apply.

Excavation and restoration can materially change the total

Exterior pier locations may require removal of soil, landscaping, hardscape or other obstacles. Interior pier locations may require slab demolition and later concrete repair.

If excavation is a large part of the quote, compare our exterior foundation excavation cost guide.

Engineering and independent review

Large underpinning projects can benefit from a clearly documented movement pattern and repair objective. If proposals differ sharply in pier count, system or affected area, an independent structural evaluation can help clarify what each bid is trying to solve.

See our structural engineer foundation cost guide and foundation inspection cost guide.

What a detailed pier proposal should show

  • Pier type and manufacturer/system where relevant.
  • Total pier count and approximate locations.
  • Affected foundation length.
  • Installation completion criteria or design basis.
  • Excavation and concrete work.
  • Engineering and permits.
  • Stabilization versus lifting objective.
  • Plumbing or utility checks if applicable.
  • Backfill, landscaping and finish restoration.
  • Warranty terms and who services the warranty.

Questions to ask when a contractor recommends piers

  • What movement or elevation data supports the recommendation?
  • Why is this pier type appropriate?
  • How many piers are included and why?
  • Where will each pier be installed?
  • How is installation completion verified?
  • Are engineering, permits, excavation and restoration included?
  • Is the goal stabilization, lifting or both?
  • What happens if additional support points are recommended after work begins?

Compare the whole project, not a single number

The cheapest per-pier quote may exclude work that another contractor includes. Compare pier count, system, access, engineering, excavation, lifting, restoration and warranty before deciding that one proposal is less expensive.

Frequently asked questions

Why do contractors price per pier?

Each support point adds material and labor, but project-level costs still affect the total.

Can two bids use different pier counts?

Yes. Ask each contractor to show the repair area and support layout so you understand the difference.

Is a low price per pier automatically a good deal?

No. Check what the quote excludes before comparing unit prices.

Are push piers and helical piers the same?

No. They use different installation approaches and can fit different project conditions.
