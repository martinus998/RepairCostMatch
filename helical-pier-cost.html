2026 HELICAL PIER COST GUIDE

How much do helical piers cost?

Helical piers are one method used to stabilize or support structures when the repair design calls for deeper support. Contractors often discuss pricing per pier, but the total project cost depends on pier count, shaft depth, helix configuration, access, structural loads, engineering and whether lifting or related repairs are included.

Current 2026 cost guides commonly place residential structural helical piers around $2,000 to $4,000 per pier. Angi uses about $3,000 per pier as a planning figure in several 2026 foundation-repair guides, while HomeGuide lists an average structural repair range of roughly $2,000 to $4,000 per pier.

2026 helical pier cost benchmarks

HomeGuide's 2026 helical pier guide lists structural foundation installations at about $2,000 to $4,000 per pier. Angi's 2026 foundation and underpinning guides commonly use about $3,000 per helical pier when discussing deeper support systems.

These figures are useful for budgeting but should not be treated as a property-specific quote. The number of piers, depth, load requirements, access and finish restoration can move the project total substantially.

Why the cost per pier can be misleading

A quote for eight piers at one unit price is not directly comparable with a quote for twelve piers at another. The total scope can also differ in shaft configuration, bracket type, engineering, monitoring, excavation, lifting and restoration.

Before comparing dollar totals, ask both contractors to explain the proposed pier count and the reason for each location. Our foundation repair cost per pier guide explains why unit pricing should always be read with the full scope.

Main helical pier cost drivers

  • Pier count: more support locations increase materials, labor, excavation and restoration.
  • Depth: deeper installation can require additional shaft sections and more installation time.
  • Helix and shaft design: project-specific configuration can affect material cost.
  • Access: decks, porches, landscaping, tight side yards and interior locations can make equipment access more difficult.
  • Engineering: structural design, load requirements and permit documentation may be separate.
  • Lifting: stabilization and attempted elevation recovery are different scopes.
  • Restoration: concrete, patios, landscaping or interior finishes may need repair after installation.

Torque and installation criteria

Helical pier proposals often reference torque, depth, load or other acceptance criteria. Those criteria matter because they help define when the pier has reached the design requirement. Ask what the project specification requires and how the contractor will document the result.

Do not compare proposals only by promised depth. Different sites and designs can call for different installation criteria, so the meaningful comparison is whether the repair follows the project design.

Helical piers versus push piers

Helical piers and push piers are not the same system. Helical piers use screw-like helical plates and are advanced into the soil. Push piers are driven using the structure as reaction weight. Both can be used for foundation support in appropriate situations.

The right choice depends on the structure, footing, soil, access and engineering design. If another contractor proposes steel push piers, compare our push pier cost guide before deciding based only on unit price.

Stabilization versus lifting

Some helical-pier projects are designed only to stabilize the foundation and reduce future movement. Others include an attempt to recover some elevation. That difference can affect equipment, monitoring, risk and project time.

Ask the contractor to state the objective clearly. The word “repair” does not automatically mean the structure will be returned to its original elevation.

Excavation and access

Helical-pier installation usually requires localized access at each support point. Patios, porches, decks, landscaping and narrow side yards can increase labor and restoration cost. If the project requires broader excavation around the foundation, see our exterior foundation excavation cost guide.

Engineering, permits and documentation

Ask whether the quote includes structural engineering, permit drawings, pre-repair elevation measurements and final installation documentation. Some contractors package those services; others list them separately.

When the cause of movement is uncertain or several repair methods are being proposed, an independent structural evaluation can help clarify the scope. See our structural engineer foundation cost guide.

Warranty and transfer terms

A long warranty headline does not tell you what is actually covered. Ask whether the warranty applies to the pier hardware, labor, the supported area, future adjustment or only specific failure conditions. Also ask about transfer requirements if the property is sold.

Compare exclusions as carefully as the warranty term itself.

Common exclusions to check

  • Structural engineering or stamped design
  • Building permits
  • Additional shaft sections if deeper installation is required
  • Footing repair or modification
  • Concrete, patio or landscape restoration
  • Interior finish repairs after movement
  • Drainage or water-control work
  • Long-term elevation monitoring

Questions to ask before signing

  • How many piers are included and how were the locations selected?
  • What installation criteria will be used?
  • What happens if more depth or shaft sections are needed?
  • Is stabilization only planned, or will lifting be attempted?
  • Are engineering and permits included?
  • Are brackets, excavation, backfill and restoration included?
  • What measurements are taken before and after installation?
  • How is the warranty structured?
  • What does the warranty exclude?
  • Is the warranty transferable?

How to compare two helical pier bids

Put the estimates side by side and compare pier count, design criteria, engineering, excavation, lifting objective, restoration, monitoring and warranty. A lower per-pier price can become the higher total when more piers or additional line items are required.
