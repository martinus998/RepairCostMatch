2026 DRAINAGE COST GUIDE

How much does foundation drainage repair cost?

Foundation drainage is not one repair. The scope can range from correcting surface water flow to installing drains, sump equipment or exterior waterproofing. The first step is identifying where the water comes from, how it reaches the foundation and where it needs to go.

When comparing bids, follow the complete water path. A proposal should explain the source, collection method, discharge route and restoration work rather than listing only a drain or membrane product.

Common drainage scopes

  • Grading: reshaping soil can help move surface water away from the foundation. See our foundation grading cost guide.
  • Gutters and downspouts: roof water may need longer discharge paths or corrected drainage.
  • Interior drainage: perimeter channels or drain tile can collect water that reaches the basement. See our basement drain tile cost guide.
  • Exterior drainage: excavation may be required for certain drain or waterproofing systems. Compare our foundation French drain cost guide.
  • Sump systems: pumps and discharge lines can be part of a drainage strategy where collected water needs mechanical removal.
  • Exterior excavation: projects that expose a long section of foundation wall can require major digging and restoration. See exterior foundation excavation cost.

Why the water source matters

A wet basement after heavy rain may involve surface drainage, groundwater, wall penetrations, cracks or several causes at once. Paying for a single product before the water path is understood can lead to incomplete work.

If water appears after storms, start with our water in basement after rain guide. If the problem is mainly below grade, compare interior and exterior drainage methods instead of assuming one system fits every property.

Surface water and groundwater are different problems

Surface runoff can often be influenced by grading, gutters and downspouts. Groundwater or water collecting below the slab may require a collection system and sump pump. Some homes have both issues at the same time.

Ask the contractor to state which water source the proposed system is designed to manage. A complete bid should connect the diagnosis to the proposed repair.

Interior drainage versus exterior drainage

Interior drainage usually involves opening the basement perimeter, installing a collection system and routing water to a sump or outlet. Exterior drainage can require excavation around the foundation, drain installation, waterproofing and substantial restoration.

Interior systems can be less disruptive outside but do not stop water from reaching the foundation wall. Exterior work may address the wall and surrounding soil directly but can require much more access and excavation.

Drainage versus waterproofing

Drainage manages where water goes. Waterproofing aims to reduce or stop water entry through the foundation wall or assembly. These are related but different scopes.

For exterior wall membranes and sealing, compare our foundation waterproofing cost guide. For the broader basement system, see basement waterproofing cost.

Sump equipment and discharge routing

If collected water cannot leave by gravity, a sump basin and pump may be part of the system. Compare the basin, primary pump, electrical work, backup power, alarms and discharge line separately.

The discharge point matters. Water released too close to the foundation can contribute to repeat moisture problems. Ask where the pipe terminates, how far it runs and whether freezing or erosion is a concern.

Cost factors to compare

Key price drivers include excavation depth, total drain length, access for equipment, soil and landscaping, interior demolition, pump equipment, electrical work, discharge routing, waterproofing membranes, permits and restoration after the drainage work is complete.

When excavation is part of the quote, ask whether soil removal, haul-away, backfill, compaction and landscaping restoration are included. Those items can materially change the total even before the drain or waterproofing system itself is priced.

What a detailed estimate should show

  • Which water source the system is intended to manage.
  • Drain length and location.
  • Interior demolition or exterior excavation.
  • Sump basin, pump and backup equipment.
  • Electrical work.
  • Discharge route and outlet location.
  • Waterproofing membrane or drainage board if included.
  • Backfill, concrete patching or landscape restoration.
  • Cleanouts or service-access points.
  • Warranty and maintenance responsibilities.

Questions to ask contractors

  • Where is the water entering or accumulating?
  • Is the proposed system managing surface water, groundwater, or both?
  • Where will collected water discharge?
  • Does the quote include excavation, restoration and electrical work?
  • Is waterproofing part of the same scope or separate?
  • Is a sump pump required?
  • How will the system be serviced if it clogs or the pump fails?
  • What happens if additional water-entry points are discovered?

Drainage and structural problems can overlap

Water and soil conditions can exist alongside settlement, cracks or wall movement. If structural repair is also being recommended, compare the drainage scope separately from the foundation repair cost so each contractor is solving the same problem.

If wall movement is part of the concern, the repair team may need to coordinate drainage with structural work rather than treating them as unrelated projects.

How to compare two drainage bids

Compare the diagnosis, drain length, collection method, sump equipment, discharge route, excavation, waterproofing, restoration and warranty. A lower total may simply omit major parts of the water-management system.

Frequently asked questions

Is foundation drainage the same as waterproofing?

No. Drainage moves water; waterproofing limits water entry. Many projects use both.

Is interior drainage the same as an exterior French drain?

No. Interior systems are installed from inside the basement, while exterior drainage requires work outside the foundation.

Why does discharge routing matter?

Collected water needs to leave the foundation area reliably or it can contribute to recurring moisture.

Should drainage and structural repairs be coordinated?

Yes when water or soil conditions exist alongside settlement, cracks or wall movement.

This guide is for planning only. Water intrusion and structural movement require an on-site evaluation to identify the correct repair method.
