2026 FLOOR STRUCTURE COST GUIDE

How much does floor joist repair cost?

Floor joist repair can range from a localized reinforcement job to a much larger structural project involving several joists, beams, subfloor removal and moisture correction. That is why a single online price can be misleading.

The most important distinction is whether the contractor is reinforcing usable framing, replacing damaged material or correcting a support problem that is causing the joists to sag.

Common repair approaches

  • Sistering: adding new framing alongside an existing joist to increase strength or stiffness.
  • Partial replacement: removing damaged sections when deterioration is localized and an appropriate repair detail is possible.
  • Full joist replacement: replacing framing that is too deteriorated or compromised to retain.
  • Beam or support correction: addressing the girder, posts or supports beneath the joists when the framing problem is part of a larger load-path issue.
  • Moisture correction: fixing drainage, humidity or water intrusion when rot or recurring dampness contributed to damage.

What usually drives the price

Access is a major factor. Tight crawl spaces, plumbing, HVAC ducts and wiring can make framing work slower. The number of joists, span length, temporary shoring, permit requirements, engineering, subfloor or finished-floor removal and the need to correct moisture all affect the final scope.

Joist repair and sagging floors are related, but not identical

A sagging floor can come from joists, but it can also come from a beam, post, pier, settlement or several issues at once. If the symptom is uneven or bouncy flooring and the cause is still unclear, start with our sagging floor repair cost guide.

If the problem is in a crawl space, also compare crawl space beam repair cost and crawl space foundation repair cost so you can see whether the bids are addressing the same structural layer.

Questions to ask before accepting a bid

  • Which joists are damaged and what caused the damage?
  • Is the contractor sistering, replacing or merely leveling?
  • Will temporary shoring be required?
  • Is subfloor or finish-floor removal included?
  • Does the moisture or support cause need separate work?
  • Are engineering, permits and cleanup included?

Do not repair the symptom and ignore the cause

If a joist failed because of chronic moisture, a leaking system or inadequate support, replacing wood alone may not solve the whole problem. A good scope should explain both the damaged framing and the condition that contributed to it.
