How much does sill plate repair cost?

Sill plate work can range from a small localized repair to a structural project that requires temporary support of part of the wall. The visible rot is only one part of the scope; access, load transfer and hidden damage around the rim area can matter just as much.

What the contractor may need to repair

  • Localized sill damage: a short damaged section may be replaced when surrounding framing is sound.
  • Longer sill sections: more extensive deterioration can require staged replacement and temporary support.
  • Rim or band joist damage: moisture or pests may affect framing next to the sill. For this specific scope, see our rim joist repair cost guide.
  • Floor joists: joist ends can also be damaged where they meet the perimeter.
  • Moisture source: drainage, leaks or crawl-space humidity may need correction so the repair lasts.

Why access changes the price

A sill plate is located where the wall framing meets the foundation. Limited crawl-space height, exterior finishes, plumbing, ducts, wiring and tight corners can make the work slower. Some jobs also require temporary shoring so damaged material can be removed without leaving the structure unsupported.

Look beyond the sill itself

Ask whether the inspection found damage in the rim joist, floor joists, studs or nearby supports. If the floor also sags, compare our sagging floor repair cost guide. If damaged joists are part of the scope, see floor joist repair cost.

Questions to ask before approving the bid

  • How many linear feet of sill plate are damaged?
  • Will the wall or floor need temporary support?
  • Is the rim joist or any joist end also damaged?
  • What caused the deterioration?
  • Does the quote include moisture correction, pest treatment, permits and finish restoration?

When crawl-space work is part of the project

If the sill damage is connected with broader crawl-space deterioration, use our crawl space foundation repair cost guide to compare support, beam, joist and moisture scopes separately.

This guide is for planning only. Structural safety and the correct repair method require an on-site evaluation.
