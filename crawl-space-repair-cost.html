2026 CRAWL SPACE COST GUIDE

How much does crawl space repair cost?

Crawl space repair is not one standardized project. The same space can need structural support, wood replacement, drainage, mold cleanup, vapor control, encapsulation or several of those together. The useful estimate starts by separating those scopes.

Current 2026 consumer cost guides from Angi and HomeAdvisor place many crawl-space repair projects around the mid-four-figure range, while also showing a much wider low-to-high spread for minor versus complex work. Treat national figures as planning context, not a quote for a specific house.

Start by identifying which crawl-space problem you actually have

Structural movement

Uneven floors, weak supports, settlement or damaged beams can require posts, beams, joists or foundation work.

Wood rot

Rotted joists, sill plates, rim joists or beams can turn a moisture problem into a structural repair.

Standing water or drainage

Surface water, groundwater, poor discharge or plumbing leaks can require different solutions.

Humidity and moisture

Vapor barriers, encapsulation and dehumidification are moisture-control scopes, not substitutes for structural repair.

Mold or contamination

Cleanup can be a separate line item and the moisture source still needs to be corrected.

Combined repair

The highest totals often occur when structural, water-control and cleanup work are bundled into one project.

Structural crawl-space repair costs

If the problem is support or movement, compare the exact component being repaired. A support-post adjustment is a different job from replacing a beam, sistering joists or stabilizing the foundation.

  • Crawl-space foundation repair cost — settlement, leveling and structural support.
  • Crawl-space support post cost — posts, footings and spacing.
  • Crawl-space beam repair cost — localized versus larger beam work.
  • Floor joist repair cost and joist sistering cost — damaged or weakened floor framing.
  • Sill plate repair cost and rim joist repair cost — perimeter framing repairs.

Moisture and water-control costs

Water control should be defined by the source. A vapor barrier handles ground moisture differently from a drainage system that manages bulk water. A dehumidifier controls air moisture but does not repair a broken drain or foundation leak.

  • Crawl-space moisture repair cost — the broad moisture overview.
  • Crawl-space drainage cost — collection, drain routing and discharge.
  • Crawl-space sump pump cost — pit, pump, discharge and backup options.
  • Crawl-space vapor barrier cost — ground vapor control.
  • Crawl-space encapsulation cost — a more complete sealed moisture-control system.
  • Crawl-space dehumidifier cost — humidity management after bulk-water issues are addressed.

Wood rot and mold can create separate scopes

Moisture can damage framing and create conditions that support mold growth. Cleanup alone does not restore damaged structural wood, and replacing wood without correcting moisture can allow the same problem to return.

Compare crawl-space wood rot repair cost and crawl-space mold remediation cost as separate scopes before combining them into one bid.

Why two crawl-space quotes can be thousands of dollars apart

  • Different diagnosis: one contractor may quote moisture control while another includes structural work.
  • Different quantities: beam length, joist count, support count and affected square footage matter.
  • Different water strategy: vapor barrier, drain, sump pump, grading and encapsulation solve different problems.
  • Access: low clearance, finished areas and difficult material handling can increase labor.
  • Hidden damage: rot or mold can become clearer after insulation or old material is removed.
  • Engineering and permits: structural work may require additional professional or local review.
  • Cleanup and restoration: debris removal, insulation and finish work may or may not be included.

What a useful crawl-space estimate should list

A detailed proposal should identify the problem, affected components, repair method, quantity, moisture-control work, cleanup, exclusions and warranty. If structural and moisture work are both present, ask for them as separate line items so you can compare competing bids fairly.

A practical order for planning the repair

1. Document the symptoms

Photograph water, damaged wood, supports, visible movement and affected floor areas.

2. Identify the water source

Separate plumbing leaks, surface drainage, groundwater and humidity before choosing a moisture system.

3. Define structural damage

Determine which beams, joists, posts or foundation elements actually require work.

4. Compare equivalent scopes

Make sure each contractor is pricing the same quantities, systems and restoration work.

Questions to ask a crawl-space contractor

  • What do you believe caused the problem?
  • Which structural components are damaged?
  • What evidence supports replacing rather than repairing each component?
  • How will water or moisture be controlled after the repair?
  • Is mold cleanup included or separate?
  • Are permits, engineering, debris removal and restoration included?
  • What is excluded from the price?
  • What conditions could increase the final cost after work begins?

Frequently asked questions

Is crawl-space repair the same as encapsulation?

No. Encapsulation manages moisture. Structural, drainage, plumbing, mold and wood problems may require separate work.

Can I repair rotten wood without fixing moisture?

The moisture source should be addressed as part of the plan so the repaired wood is not exposed to the same condition again.

Why are crawl-space cost ranges so wide?

Because “crawl-space repair” can describe anything from a localized component repair to a multi-system structural and water-control project.

Should I get an engineer?

An independent structural opinion can be useful when movement is significant, repair proposals differ sharply or a major structural project is recommended.
