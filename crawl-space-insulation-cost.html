Crawl space insulation cost in 2026

Crawl space insulation pricing varies widely because the project can range from installing basic insulation in an accessible dry space to combining insulation with removal, air sealing, vapor control, drainage or full encapsulation. Treat the insulation quote as one part of the crawl space system rather than an isolated line item.

2026 crawl space insulation cost range

Angi's 2026 crawl space insulation cost guide reports a typical professional range of about $2,600 to $16,900, with an average around $3,550 for a 1,000-square-foot crawl space. The same guide notes that material and labor costs vary substantially by insulation type, site conditions and the amount of prep work required.

That range is useful for early planning, but it is not a contractor quote. A dry, open crawl space with straightforward access may be very different from a low-clearance space with old insulation, moisture damage or extensive air-sealing work.

What changes the price most?

  • Square footage: more wall or floor area means more material and labor.
  • Insulation type: fiberglass, rigid foam and spray foam have different material and installation costs.
  • Access: low clearance, obstructions and tight utility areas can slow installation.
  • Removal: damaged, wet or contaminated old insulation may need to be removed first.
  • Moisture: leaks, standing water, drainage problems or high humidity may require separate work.
  • Air sealing: rim joists, penetrations and crawl space walls may need sealing before insulation performs well.
  • Encapsulation: adding a sealed ground vapor barrier, dehumidification or drainage can substantially increase the project scope.

Insulation and moisture should be planned together

The U.S. Department of Energy has published building-science guidance showing that crawl space moisture control is important to durability and comfort. In humid climates, traditional vented crawl spaces can bring moist outdoor air into a cooler crawl space, where condensation can contribute to mold and wood damage. DOE guidance also emphasizes ground vapor control, drainage and correct treatment of crawl space walls and mechanical systems.

That means insulation should not be used to hide an existing moisture problem. Before accepting an insulation-only proposal, identify whether the space has bulk water, plumbing leaks, poor grading, inadequate drainage, high humidity or wood damage.

Department of Energy crawl space guidance explains why sealed and conditioned crawl spaces can perform differently from traditional vented spaces.

Insulation versus encapsulation

Insulation controls heat flow. Encapsulation is a broader moisture and air-control strategy. Depending on the home and climate, a crawl space project may include both. If you are comparing proposals, make sure each contractor is pricing the same scope.

Do not ignore structural or wood damage

If the crawl space has sagging floors, soft joists, visible rot or failing beams, insulation is not the first repair to price. Structural work can be a separate project. Use the crawl space beam repair cost, floor joist repair cost, wood rot repair cost and support post cost guides to separate those scopes.

Questions to ask before comparing insulation bids

  • What insulation material and R-value are included?
  • Is the quote for the crawl space floor, walls, rim joists or a combination?
  • Does the price include removal and disposal of old insulation?
  • Will the contractor address visible moisture before installation?
  • Is air sealing included?
  • Is a ground vapor barrier included or priced separately?
  • Are drainage, dehumidification or mold work excluded?
  • Does the proposal include cleanup, permits or access work?
