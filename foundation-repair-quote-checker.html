Check a foundation repair quote before you sign

A foundation repair proposal can look complete while still leaving important details unclear. Use this checklist to compare scope, method, payment terms and warranty before you decide whether two quotes are actually comparable.

Use the free Foundation Repair Quote Comparison Calculator to place the totals and optional pier counts side by side. It shows arithmetic differences without ranking contractors or pretending the scopes are identical.

Do not compare only the final price

A $7,000 proposal and a $12,000 proposal may not be offering the same repair. One might include more piers, engineering, drainage correction, excavation, permits or restoration. Compare the written scope line by line before deciding that one contractor is simply more expensive.

Six questions to ask every contractor

  • What exactly is causing the movement, cracking or water problem?
  • Why is this repair method appropriate for the problem?
  • How many piers, anchors, beams, drains or other units are included?
  • Is the goal stabilization, lifting, waterproofing, cosmetic repair or a combination?
  • What could increase the price after work starts?
  • What does the written warranty cover and exclude?

Deposits and payment terms

A deposit is not automatically a problem, but the amount, timing and refund terms should be clear. Ask when each payment becomes due and what milestone triggers it. Avoid relying on verbal promises that are not reflected in the written proposal.

Engineering, permits and inspections

Large structural repairs may involve engineering, permits or inspections depending on the scope and local rules. Confirm who is responsible for arranging and paying for each item. If contractors propose very different structural solutions, an independent professional opinion can help separate diagnosis from sales.

When to slow down before signing

  • The repair method is not explained.
  • The proposal has a large price but no quantities.
  • Engineering or permit responsibility is unclear.
  • The contractor will not provide written warranty terms.
  • Major drainage or excavation work is discussed but excluded from the written scope.
  • Two contractors diagnose completely different problems.
RepairCostMatch provides educational planning information. It is not a structural engineering opinion, contractor recommendation or legal review of a contract.
