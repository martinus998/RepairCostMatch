How much does basement wall crack injection cost?

Crack injection is commonly discussed for poured-concrete basement walls when a limited crack needs to be sealed or bonded from the interior. The installed scope depends on crack length, wall access, whether water is actively entering, surface preparation and whether the crack appears stable.

Injection is a repair method for the crack itself. It should not be confused with stabilizing a wall that is bowing or a foundation that is settling.

Two common injection approaches

  • Epoxy injection: often used where bonding a stable concrete crack is part of the repair goal.
  • Polyurethane injection: often selected where flexibility and water sealing are important, depending on the crack and contractor system.

The right material depends on the condition and repair objective. Ask why one system is being recommended rather than choosing by price alone.

Crack access can change the project

A visible unfinished concrete wall is easier to prepare than a crack hidden behind drywall, insulation, shelving or finished trim. Finished-wall removal and later restoration may be separate line items.

Ask whether the estimate includes access work, cleanup and finish restoration so two bids cover the same scope.

Active leakage needs more than a material choice

If the crack leaks during rain, document when and where water appears. Injection may address the entry point, but exterior drainage, grading or water pressure can still contribute to recurring moisture.

For the broader water path, compare our water in basement after rain guide, foundation drainage repair cost and basement waterproofing cost.

Injection is not structural stabilization

A crack can be sealed while the underlying wall or foundation continues to move. Horizontal cracks, stair-step patterns, displacement, bowing, repeated reopening or cracking combined with sloping floors can justify a broader structural evaluation.

For broader pricing, see our foundation crack repair cost guide. If the wall is moving inward, compare bowing basement wall repair cost.

What a detailed injection estimate should show

  • Crack location and approximate length.
  • Whether active leakage is present.
  • Epoxy, polyurethane or another proposed material.
  • Surface preparation.
  • Ports, injection and finish steps.
  • Drywall or finish removal.
  • Cleanup and restoration.
  • Warranty for leakage or reopening.
  • Any drainage work included or excluded.

Why multiple cracks can change the scope

Several nearby cracks may be separate shrinkage cracks, or they may be part of a broader movement pattern. Ask whether the contractor sees them as independent repairs or evidence of a larger condition.

If several crack types appear together, review our foundation crack guide before comparing estimates.

When an independent opinion can help

If one contractor recommends injection and another recommends wall reinforcement, underpinning or another major structural system, an independent evaluation can help clarify whether the crack is stable and what the repair objective should be.

See our structural engineer foundation cost guide for that scope.

Questions to ask the contractor

  • Do you believe this crack is stable?
  • Why are you recommending epoxy or polyurethane?
  • Is the goal bonding, water sealing or both?
  • Does the price include finished-wall removal and restoration?
  • Is drainage or waterproofing work needed too?
  • What happens if the crack moves again?
  • What does the warranty cover?
  • Do you see signs that require structural repair instead?

Frequently asked questions

Is epoxy the same as polyurethane?

No. They have different properties and may be selected for different repair goals.

Can injection fix a moving foundation?

No. Injection treats the crack but does not correct active settlement or wall movement.

Should drainage be checked if the crack leaks?

Yes. Exterior water conditions may contribute to repeated leakage.

What if the crack reopens?

Ask what the warranty covers and whether reopening would indicate continued movement that needs a different repair.
